# Round 3, deadline day: stochasticity and the hidden-point task (2026-11-22, Daniel; Seth's lead: "the analysis around the stochasticness of the data when trying to improve recall")

## What the morning's CPU check found (receipts not published; superseded by `results/round3/`, scripts `harness/ceiling_lateral.py`, `harness/ceiling_cv.py`)

1. **The neighbour rule on real windows sits on the random walk's closed form.** Rule: the hidden point is above its left visible
   neighbour iff the right visible neighbour is. For a random walk with the hidden point k steps right of the left neighbour and m
   steps left of the right one, P(rule right) = 1 − arccos(√(k/(k+m)))/π. Over all 6,722 real 36-month windows (109 series):
   S1 (3,3) 0.742 vs 0.750; S2 (3,1) 0.820 vs 0.833; S3 (1,1) 0.741 vs 0.750. Series-cluster bootstrap CIs of the difference
   include or touch zero at every scale.
2. **The LM's best arm on real windows reaches the rule at S1 (0.75) and S3 (0.74), and falls short at S2 (0.75 vs 0.82).**
3. **THE INSTRUMENT LEAKS (mine to report; it passed two cold reviews).** `make_items` standardised each window over all 36 points,
   the hidden ones included, so the scaled values sum to zero and the hidden point at stage 3 equals minus the sum of the visible
   ones. A one-line reader gets 0.94 on real and 1.00 on synth at stage 3 from that alone. A logistic reader found it (0.83 on
   real S3 against the walk's 0.75). **The LM did not** (0.74 on real S3, 0.87 agreement with the neighbour rule). Every lateral
   number from 11-19 and 11-20 was measured on the leaking instrument; none may travel until re-measured.
4. **A second flaw: the real split is not held out by series.** 298 of the 300 non-WTI eval windows come from series that are also
   in training (windows step by 4 months and overlap).
